Streaming payments for Monero

Pay by the block, not by the invoice.

Lock XMR once. It drips to one or more recipients every ~2 minute block until the stream ends or you cancel. Salaries, grants, rent and vesting, settled in native Monero and visible only to the parties involved.

The flow

From one deposit to many payouts.

01

Fund

Send XMR from your own wallet to a stream address. Your seed never leaves your device.

02

Define

Pick recipients, their shares, and a duration in blocks. The schedule is fixed at creation.

03

Stream

Each new block releases deposit ÷ duration. Released funds belong to recipients immediately.

04

Withdraw

Recipients sweep their released balance to any Monero address whenever they like.

05

Cancel

The sender can stop the stream. Unreleased XMR returns; already released XMR stays with recipients.

Uses

Anything paid over time.

Payroll

Contributors see money arrive continuously instead of waiting for month end. Stop a stream the moment someone leaves.

Grants and bounties

Trust model

What you give up, and what you keep.

Your wallet keys stay yours.
Funding a stream is an ordinary Monero transaction from your own wallet. XMRFLOW never asks for a seed or view key.
Streams run in a reserve.
Locked XMR sits in a 2-of-3 multisig held by the sender, the operator and an independent keeper. No single party can move it.
Release is a ledger entry, not a transaction.
Per-block releases update the stream ledger. Only withdrawals create on-chain Monero transactions, so recipients pay fees once, not every block.
Privacy boundaries are explicit.
The operator sees stream amounts and schedules. The Monero network sees only ordinary ring-signature transactions in and out.
Is this custodial?

Partly. Locked funds are in a multisig the sender co-controls, so the operator cannot run off with them, but the operator can stall withdrawals. Treat it as a service with published reserve rules, not as a trustless protocol.

Can a stream be sped up?

No. The release rate is fixed at creation. The only changes allowed are cancel (sender) and withdraw (recipient).

What happens on a reorg?

Releases follow the longest chain. A release counted at height N is only treated as final after 10 confirmations, matching Monero's own unlock rule.

Why blocks and not seconds?

Block height is the one clock every party can verify. Monero targets 120 seconds per block, so 720 blocks is a day in practice, but the schedule is defined in blocks.

XMRFLOW is a design concept. There is no deployed service, token or program behind this page, and nothing on it is financial advice.
